## What legacy Pro actually gives you

Two pools that drain independently is the detail most comparisons skip. Your Cursor Models allowance (Grok 4.5 through 4.7, Composer 2.5) and your Other Models allowance (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and others) empty at their own pace, so one can sit at zero while the other still has room. Before paying for a bigger tier, open the Spending tab and check which pool ran out. If it's the third-party one, moving routine work onto Cursor's own models may stretch the plan you already have.

Pro includes unlimited Tab completions, access to frontier models, a Grok allowance, MCPs, skills and hooks, and Cloud Agents. Bugbot is available on the plan but billed by usage, so its presence on the feature list doesn't mean unlimited reviews. A third-party guide verified against Cursor's pricing page in late September puts Pro+ at roughly **3x Pro's limits** and Ultra at **20x**; Cursor itself publishes no such multiples, so treat those as estimates.

In-house model rates, per million tokens, explain how fast a pool drains far better than a request count:

- **Grok 4.7**: $2 input, $0.50 cache read, $6 output. Fast mode doubles all three to $4 / $1 / $12.
- **Composer 2.5**: $0.50 input, $0.20 cache read, $2.50 output. Fast mode is $3 / $0.50 / $15.
- Anything above 256k input tokens bills at **2x** standard rates and caps at 500k. Long context combined with Fast bills at **3x**.

Auto mode no longer means one thing either. It now splits into Cost, Balance and Intelligence routing, and individual plans only get Cost mode, which bills as a Cursor-model request drawing from the usage pool whatever model actually serves it. Balance and Intelligence, where the routed model's list price applies, are a Teams and Enterprise feature.

## Switching, downgrading and refunds

Switching to The X Subscription is a one-way door. Cursor cancels the legacy subscription with a prorated refund, you pick a plan on the new bundle, and the change is permanent. Your Cursor and Grok accounts get combined in the process.

If you stay legacy, downgrades are scheduled rather than immediate: choose the lower tier in the billing dashboard, confirm, and the plan holds until the current cycle ends. Upgrades apply at once, and any credit for the old plan is based on its unused included usage, not the days remaining in the period. Yearly plans can't convert to monthly mid-term; the change is queued for the end of the annual period.

On refunds, a third-party guide states Cursor may refund your most recent Pro, Pro+, or Ultra payment within 14 days provided you haven't used the plan in that window, with consumed on-demand charges excluded. That specific text didn't appear on the official pricing page checked for this article, so confirm it with support before relying on it.

Two more behaviours worth knowing. Legacy pools reset with your monthly billing cycle and unused usage doesn't roll over, so buying a bigger tier for one busy month doesn't build a reserve. New-bundle pools refresh weekly instead, which spreads a heavy day across the month.

## FAQ

**Can I still subscribe to Cursor Pro for $20/month?**
Not on a new account. Existing legacy Pro subscribers keep the price and features until they switch or cancel, and they can still move to Pro+ or Ultra within the legacy lineup without migrating.

**Is the $14.99 "Cursor Pro" app in the Mac App Store the same product?**
No. It's a pointer-highlighting and screen-magnifier utility by App ahead GmbH for macOS 11.5 and later, unrelated to the editor.

**What's the closest replacement for Pro?**
Premium at $30/mo, which carries 4x Plus usage plus all the agent and third-party features Pro had. Plus at $8/mo is the cheapest paid entry point.

**Does Plus include Claude or GPT models?**
No. Third-party models start at Premium, billed at API list price from prepaid credit.

**Is there a free trial?**
Cursor doesn't advertise one. The Free plan is a standing free tier with limited usage of Agent, Chat and Tab on Cursor's own models.

**How do I know how much usage is left?**
Remaining usage and the next reset date appear in the editor's settings and in the dashboard's Usage and Spending views, broken down by pool on legacy plans.
